How the internet physically works

1. Radio: your phone talks to a cell tower (or Wi-Fi router).
2. Access fibre: the tower is wired to your ISP's network.
3. Data centre / exchange: ISPs, clouds and CDNs physically interconnect in buildings like Equinix or Telehouse. Traffic is handed over via BGP routing.
4. Landing station: long-haul fibre reaches the coast at a cable landing station.
5. Submarine cable: light pulses in fibre pairs cross the ocean, boosted by repeaters about every 50-100 km. About 99% of intercontinental traffic uses these cables, owned by consortia of telecoms and tech companies.
6. Repeat in reverse at the other end until the destination server answers.
Cables and landings: TeleGeography Submarine Cable Map (CC BY-NC-SA 3.0, non-commercial). Data centres: PeeringDB. Towers: OpenStreetMap contributors. Basemaps: Esri, HERE, Garmin, OpenStreetMap contributors.
